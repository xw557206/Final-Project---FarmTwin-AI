Section 1 · Title and links

Executive summary and evidence position

FarmTwin AI is a local-first farm management workbench for a fictional Connecticut demonstration farm. It combines a visual farm map, production records, weather intelligence, deterministic finance and pricing, inventory traceability, buyer matching, dairy operations, poultry and egg operations, and a bounded AI advisor.

The project’s strongest demonstrated contribution is architectural: it keeps business arithmetic and state changes in deterministic backend services while placing natural-language interpretation behind an allowlisted, observable coordinator. The owner can inspect source status, assumptions, tool traces, and approval state before any consequential action.

Section 2 · Motivation

The decision problem is cross-functional.

Small farm owners make decisions across production, weather, labor, inventory, cost, pricing, and sales. A wholesale offer may be commercially attractive but operationally unsafe if the expected harvest is confused with available stock, a lot is near expiration, or the price falls below cost.

In a small operation, these decisions are often managed through a mix of spreadsheets, notes, weather websites, accounting records, and direct conversations. The issue is not that each source is useless. The issue is that the owner must manually reconcile them at the moment a decision matters.

Figure 1. The decision is connected even when the records are not. This is a design problem statement, not a measured industry statistic.

Target user

Owner-operators and small farm teams that need a current operating picture without an enterprise implementation.

Operational consequences

  • Perishable inventory increases the cost of delay.
  • Forecast production can be mistaken for physically available inventory.
  • Price recommendations are weak when cost and stock are disconnected.

Why build FarmTwin?

The project tests whether one local application can make those dependencies visible and auditable while keeping the owner in control of commercial action.

Evidence boundary: no farmer interviews, adoption data, or quantified industry cost estimates were collected. The motivation is a reasoned product hypothesis, not a survey finding.

Section 3 · What is new here

Integration is the differentiation, not a claim of category dominance.

Farm management platforms commonly focus on planning, records, field operations, livestock, or financial administration. General-purpose AI assistants can explain information and draft text, but they do not automatically know which farm data is authoritative or whether an action is safe.

FarmTwin’s implemented distinction is a cross-functional decision chain: the same local data model feeds the virtual farm, weather intelligence, production views, finance, pricing, inventory, buyer matching, and approval-aware drafting.

Section 4 · The agents

One coordinator, five specialist domains, deterministic authority.

Figure 2. Actual architecture. The Farm Manager is the coordinator. Weather, Production, Finance, Pricing, and Sales are the five specialist domains. Dairy and poultry are read-tool groups within those domains, not separate autonomous model loops.

Model boundary

The model may classify a request, select from the allowlisted tool registry, and synthesize a response. It is not the authority for arithmetic, inventory state, pricing state, or external communication. The browser never receives provider keys.

Provider support

OpenAI Responses is implemented for a verified configured model. A Portkey adapter expects the exact course gateway and maps to the course model. No endpoint or model mapping is invented.

Current status: no genuine provider call verified.

Backend limits

3 maximum model calls · 32 tool calls · 900 output tokens per call · 5,000 total tokens · 60 seconds per run.

Cost formula to apply after live collection

Section 6 · Safety

Safety is enforced at multiple boundaries.

FarmTwin treats buyer notes, weather text, imported data, product descriptions, and other external text as untrusted. The safety strategy does not depend on the model obeying a system prompt: tool access is allowlisted, deterministic services own state changes, and high-consequence actions remain outside the model’s authority.

Section 8 · Limits and next steps

What is ready for demonstration, and what is not.

Demonstrated in the local workspace

  • FastAPI backend, React/Vite frontend, SQLite persistence, and idempotent seed behavior.
  • Interactive crop map with original crop sprites and facility navigation.
  • Shared weather route using Open-Meteo and NWS with LIVE_FORECAST, CACHED, and UNAVAILABLE statuses.
  • Deterministic finance, pricing, inventory, traceability, dairy, poultry, and safety tooling.
  • Agent registry, bounded budgets, sanitized traces, and provider-unavailable handling.

Not yet validated for real farm use

  • Live AI model execution and provider-reported token economics.
  • Real farm data, sensor data, or verified market benchmark feeds.
  • Real-user time savings, revenue, or waste reduction.
  • Authentication and multi-user permissions.
  • Regulatory compliance for dairy or egg handling and sales.
  • Direct email, autonomous price changes, or autonomous external actions.

Data and domain limitations

Seeded crop, dairy, poultry, financial, buyer, and market records are synthetic. Weather is a coordinate-based model forecast, not a sensor reading from the farm. Crop and livestock thresholds are configurable demonstration assumptions unless a cited source is attached. Dairy and poultry features include recordkeeping and controls, not veterinary diagnosis, food-safety certification, or regulatory approval.
